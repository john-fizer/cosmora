# Skill: Sect

## Purpose
Determine whether the chart is a day or night chart and apply sect-based interpretive weighting to planets, especially the luminaries, benefics, and malefics.

## Inputs Required
- Sun's position relative to the horizon (above = day, below = night)
- Sect light (Sun for day charts, Moon for night charts)
- Benefic of sect (Jupiter for day, Venus for night)
- Malefic contrary to sect (Mars for day charts, Saturn for night charts)

## Rules

### Day Chart (Sun above horizon)
- **Sect light:** Sun — the Sun leads the chart; solar themes dominate
- **Benefic of sect:** Jupiter — Jupiter is more helpful, constructive, and expansive
- **Malefic of sect:** Saturn — Saturn is the "easier" malefic; more structure than destruction
- **Out-of-sect malefic:** Mars — Mars is more disruptive, impulsive, and harder to manage
- Venus and Moon are weakened slightly relative to day charts

### Night Chart (Sun below horizon)
- **Sect light:** Moon — the Moon leads the chart; lunar themes, instinct, and cycles dominate
- **Benefic of sect:** Venus — Venus is more helpful, pleasurable, and relational
- **Malefic of sect:** Mars — Mars is the "easier" malefic; more drive than destruction
- **Out-of-sect malefic:** Saturn — Saturn is more burdensome, limiting, and harder to resolve
- Sun and Jupiter are slightly weakened relative to night charts

## Interpretation Pattern
1. State whether this is a day or night chart and what that means.
2. Name the sect light and its condition.
3. Identify the benefic of sect and describe how it operates.
4. Identify the malefic contrary to sect and flag its themes as more challenging.
5. Note if the out-of-sect malefic is angular or strongly placed — this raises its impact.

## Guardrails
- Sect modifies planets — it does not override sign, dignity, or house.
- A day chart is not "better" than a night chart.
- Out-of-sect planets are more challenging to integrate, not inherently malevolent.
- Always combine sect analysis with the broader chart context.
